Your one-person AI studio

> make room for ideas_

Workbench puts your AI tools in one native Mac app. Bring Codex, Claude Code or any command-line agent, give each one a role, and run code, video, audio and design work from a single studio on your own machine.

The studio home: four workspaces, the people on each, and what is waiting for your review. Shown in the Pixel look.

01 · Workspaces

Four studios in one window

Each workspace has its own AI team, tasks and projects. Add your own workspaces too, with a name, icon and color.

  • Video

    Storyboards, keyframes and edits. A director agent plans shots and runs them on your GPU.

  • Audio

    Voiceover, sound design and music workflows, with waveform previews of every take.

  • Code

    Agents that read and edit your git projects, show the changed files and their diffs, and never write without your say-so.

  • Design

    Key art, posters and variations. Compare images side by side and keep the ones you like.

02 · Your AI team

Bring your own AI, give it a job

Nothing is built in. Add any command-line agent by hand (Codex, Claude Code or your own), then hire it as an “AI employee” with a role, instructions and an access level.

  • Chat in projects. Replies stream in, tool activity is listed, and every turn continues the agent's own session.
  • Read-only or Edit. You choose per agent and per message. Edit turns list the changed files with a diff.
  • Team mode. A lead agent splits the work, you edit and approve the plan, and steps run in order, one writer per folder.
  • Reviews. A reviewer agent checks each step against its acceptance criteria, with the diff as evidence.
  • Stay in control. Queue, stop or retry any turn, or continue the same session in the built-in terminal.

03 · Media studio

Direct your own GPU

Connect ComfyUI on a GPU you rent by the hour. A director agent turns a brief into a production plan, and nothing is submitted until you approve the plan and its budget.

  • On-demand servers. SSH by key or by a password kept in the Keychain. Start and stop commands, a connection test, and tunnels that reconnect on their own.
  • Templates. Import any API-format workflow and choose which inputs the director may set.
  • Budgets. Caps in GPU minutes or money. The plan pauses before a job would go over.
  • Reviews. The director looks at the image or the keyframes and asks for a rework with new settings when needed.

05 · Local-first

Your studio stays on your Mac

  • No account

    No sign-up and no telemetry. Workbench only talks to the CLIs and servers you set up yourself.

  • Your data

    Projects, conversations and runs live in a local database, with a backup before every upgrade.

  • Your keys

    Tokens, SSH passwords and secret variables go to the macOS Keychain, never into files or exports.

  • Your logins

    Each CLI runs with its own sign-in on your machine. Media jobs go only to the servers you add.

06 · Install

Get started in a minute

  1. 1
    Download Workbench-1.0.0.dmg, open it and drag Workbench to Applications.
  2. 2
    Open it the first time. This build is not notarized by Apple yet, so macOS stops the first launch. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway, or run this once in Terminal:
    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Workbench.app
  3. 3
    Add a CLI. In 工具连接, add the CLI you already use, such as Codex or Claude Code. Example definitions are ready to import.
